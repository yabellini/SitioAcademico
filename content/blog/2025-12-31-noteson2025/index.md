Another lap around the sun, another year that comes to an end. And, like, for some years now, I’m here reflecting on how 2025 went.  This is a year I want to annotate, not wrap up. I did and live a lot of things, some more visible than others. Many of them overlapped. Travel blurred into speaking. Speaking blurred into teaching. Teaching blurred into writing. Writing blurred into organizing.

These are notes I’m sharing while the year is still around and also their memory.

## On Moving Around

_Notes from being elsewhere_

I moved a lot this year.

New Orleans for The Carpentries. A board of directors retreat for bounds and institutional strategy in uncertain times. São Paulo for the [DISC Unconf by NumFOCUS]() to discuss Open Science and Open Source with a Latin American flavor. Thailand, with the [CSID Network](), to meet many new people and share how my communities can support the work of people working on climate change and infectious diseases. Durham to give a [keynote on community building and open-source software sustainability at useR!](), and San Diego for the CZI Open Science meeting.

Each trip had a purpose, but what stayed with me was how being elsewhere reshaped my attention. Travel compresses time: conversations get faster, and meeting people in a different place makes it easier to connect beyond agendas and topics. Exhaustion also arrives earlier than expected.

I noticed how much of my work depends on presence, not just being there synchronously, but being available, listening closely, noticing what isn’t said. I also noticed how much travel takes out of me now, and how intentional I need to be about when it’s worth it.

## On Speaking

_Notes from being listened to_

Speaking is a privilege. It means being among the few whose voice is given time and attention. [Speaking this year]() felt more like translating. Between communities, disciplines, languages, and expectations.

I started the year with a [keynote on lessons learned from building and translating Teaching Tech Together](), and I ended the year with [another keynote on the same underlying topic](). This circle reminded me that even when ideas are familiar and we’ve heard them many times, evidence-based knowledge still needs to be revisited, refined, and shared. Especially when we know we can do better. It also highlights the importance of reflection on our own practice. Being a keynote speaker at useR! In Durham was a moment of pause because it forced me to articulate things I often hold implicitly about community participation, sustainability, and responsibility. 

I found myself contextualizing more.
Letting ideas unfold at their own pace. These talks were meant to keep the conversation alive.

## On Teaching

_Notes from learning while teaching_

I taught [undergraduates at Austral University]() this year. Teaching students early in their careers keeps me honest, and this year has forced me to face and reflect on [how we can still teach efficiently, with our students at the center, even when genAI is around](). 

Teaching also continued in community spaces. I ran or spoke at R-Ladies meetups, where teaching is less about curriculum and more about letting learning happen socially. I also taught for the Software Sustainability Institute, [equipping researchers with digital skills for their work]().

Alongside all of this, I led the [rOpenSci Champions Program](). For the first time, we ran the entire program in Spanish! That changed many aspects; it wasn’t just a translation of content; it was a redevelopment of processes and a redistribution of support and focus.

Teaching againg in two different human languages made visible how much friction language adds, and how much disappears when people don’t have to translate themselves first.


## On Writing

_Notes from thinking on the page_

Writing was one of the ways I processed the year while it was happening. It is also part of my job. And something that is still hard to do in more than one language. 

I wrote for rOpenSci [about technical work and community building](), for [R-Ladies](), for [The Carpentries]() and for [my personal website](). Some of it was explanatory, some reflective, some intentionally experimental. 

I tried new formats: [linkedtorials, skytorials](), as a way to test how ideas move when they’re less linear. Writing felt less like producing finished pieces and more like building pathways: letting readers enter at different points, follow different threads.

I also worked on writing and revising several of [the books we maintain at rOpenSci](). That kind of writing is collective and slow. It requires letting go of ownership and paying attention to how knowledge ages and evolves. It is also very rewarding when the content improves through community contributions. 

Being interviewed twice by Nature this year, on [computational skills]() and [research](), was another reminder that writing and speaking often serve as translation layers. What sounds obvious in one community needs careful framing in another.

Writing, for me, remained a way to make thinking visible and accountable. It's always a rich source of feedback and a way to improve what I’m doing.

## On Health

_Notes from taking care of me_

This year, health was present in a calm, steady way.
I had my regular checko uts, and I’m in good shape. 

I was able to maintain my swimming and show up consistently at the gym. Two days a week for each. Nothing extreme, just rhythm. Repetition. Being consistent has been the most important ingredient in my success. ;-)

Sharing that routine with my mom gave it a different weight. It turned exercise into companionship, into something sustained together rather than optimized alone. Some weeks it was motivation; other weeks it was simply company.

Health this year wasn’t about pushing.
It was about continuity and being grateful for a body and mind that let me do the work I care about.

## The last note

One more time, putting these notes together has made me aware of how many things we did and achieved during 2025. Not everything is here; I’ve left out the struggles and the moments of uncertainty that also shaped 2025, but they’re part of the context in which all of this happened.

None of this unfolded in isolation. I leaned on the open source community, the teams and people around rOpenSci, The Carpentries, R-Ladies, and LatinR, and on my family, who supported me in ways that don’t always show up in writing.

These notes aren’t conclusions.
They’re a way of remembering who and what made this year possible.

**If you made it this far, thanks for reading. I wish you a great 2026.**
