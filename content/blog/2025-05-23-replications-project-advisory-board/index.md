About the Project
The project seeks to understand why replication studies often struggle to influence the belief in the claims they call into doubt (or support) – and to build a range of software tools to improve their visibility and increase impact. It includes three main strands:

Bibliometric analyses to identify when replications shift perceptions of original claims
Mixed-methods research to explore barriers to the use of replication findings
Tool development and evaluation to support greater visibility and use of replications
We’ve already compiled the largest database of replication studies in the social and behavioural sciences (Replication Hub) and are excited to build on this foundation."

I'm part of the the Advisory Board for our UKRI-funded project, Enhancing the Visibility and Impact of Replication Studies in Social and Behavioural Sciences (run by Lukas Wallrich and Lukas Röseler at the Münster Center of Open Science, in close collaboration with FORRT). I was invited by my experience in creating inclusive developer communities and provide insights in the planing of hackathons and work to develop a community that makes replication studies more visible to researchers, peer reviewers, and educators.

About the Project
The project seeks to understand why replication studies often struggle to influence the belief in the claims they call into doubt (or support) – and to build a range of software tools to improve their visibility and increase impact. It includes three main strands:

Bibliometric analyses to identify when replications shift perceptions of original claims
Mixed-methods research to explore barriers to the use of replication findings
Tool development and evaluation to support greater visibility and use of replications
We’ve already compiled the largest database of replication studies in the social and behavioural sciences (Replication Hub) and are excited to build on this foundation.
